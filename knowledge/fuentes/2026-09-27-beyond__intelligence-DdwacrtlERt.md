# Prompts de IA para validar ideas de startups

![[_adjuntos/DdwacrtlERt.jpg|320]]

## Qué es

El post presenta cinco prompts de IA para transformar una idea de startup en un negocio real, validando problemas y encontrando clientes.

## Ideas clave

- Usar prompts de IA para evaluar la viabilidad de una idea de startup.
- Validar el problema que la startup busca resolver.
- Identificar la competencia real de la startup.
- Conseguir los primeros 10 clientes de manera manual.
- Construir un MVP enfocado para probar la hipótesis central del negocio.

## Herramientas

- **Claude** — Refinar ideas de startups como lo haría un evaluador experto.

> **Imagen 2**
> 1. PRESSURE TEST YOUR IDEA
> Finds the assumptions and weaknesses that could kill your startup before you waste time building it.
> Prompt:
> Act as a brutally honest startup evaluator with deep experience analyzing early stage companies.
> I want you to pressure test my startup idea before I invest significant time or money into building it.
> STARTUP IDEA: [INSERT YOUR STARTUP IDEA]
> TARGET CUSTOMER: [INSERT YOUR TARGET CUSTOMER]
> BUSINESS MODEL: [INSERT HOW YOU PLAN TO MAKE MONEY]
> Analyze the idea as if your job is to find the reasons it should NOT be built.
> Follow this process:
> 1. Restate the idea in one precise sentence.
> 2. Identify the single most important assumption that must be true for this business to work.
> 3. Identify the 5 biggest risks that could kill the business.
> 4. Separate fatal problems from problems that can realistically be solved.
> 5. Examine whether the customer pain is strong enough to drive action.
> 6. Evaluate whether the proposed solution is meaningfully better than existing alternatives.
> 7. Identify what I may be assuming without evidence.
> 8. Give me the 3 fastest and cheapest tests I can run to validate the idea.
> 9. Score the idea from 1 to 10 based on the evidence available.
> 10. Give me a direct verdict: BUILD, TEST FIRST, PIVOT, or ABANDON.
> Do not give generic startup advice.
> Challenge my assumptions.
> If critical information is missing, ask me for it before making a confident conclusion.
> Output:
> Core Assumption → Biggest Risks → Hidden Assumptions → Validation Tests → Score → Verdict
> 
> **Imagen 3**
> 2. VALIDATE THE PROBLEM
> Determines whether the problem is real, painful, frequent, and important enough for customers to pay to solve.
> Prompt:
> Act as a customer research strategist specializing in early stage startups.
> Your goal is to determine whether the problem behind my startup is real, frequent, painful, and valuable enough for customers to pay to solve.
> STARTUP IDEA: [INSERT YOUR IDEA]
> TARGET CUSTOMER: [INSERT YOUR TARGET CUSTOMER]
> PROBLEM I BELIEVE THEY HAVE: [INSERT THE PROBLEM]
> CURRENT SOLUTION: [INSERT WHAT CUSTOMERS CURRENTLY DO INSTEAD]
> EXPECTED PRICE: [INSERT PRICE OR PRICING MODEL]
> Analyze this problem without assuming that my hypothesis is correct.
> 1. Define the exact problem from the customer's perspective.
> 2. Identify who experiences this problem most severely.
> 3. Determine how often the problem occurs.
> 4. Determine the financial, emotional, or operational cost.
> 5. Identify what customers currently do to solve it.
> 6. Explain why their current solution is insufficient.
> 7. Determine whether this is a must solve problem or a nice to have.
> 8. Identify the strongest evidence that would prove the problem is real.
> 9. Create 7 customer interview questions that uncover the truth without leading the customer.
> 10. Define clear validation signals that would justify building the product.
> Do not use hypothetical demand as evidence.
> Prioritize observed behavior, existing spending, frequency, urgency, and willingness to change.
> Output:
> Problem → Customer → Pain → Current Solution → Interview Questions → Validation Signals → Verdict
> 
> **Imagen 4**
> 3. FIND YOUR REAL COMPETITION
> Maps direct competitors, indirect alternatives, existing habits, manual processes, and everything else competing for the customer's attention or money.
> Prompt:
> Act as a competitive intelligence strategist for an early stage startup.
> I want you to identify everything my startup is actually competing against, including alternatives that are not traditional competitors.
> STARTUP IDEA: [INSERT YOUR IDEA]
> TARGET CUSTOMER: [INSERT TARGET CUSTOMER]
> CORE PROBLEM: [INSERT PROBLEM YOU SOLVE]
> PROPOSED SOLUTION: [INSERT YOUR SOLUTION]
> CURRENT CUSTOMER ALTERNATIVE: [INSERT WHAT CUSTOMERS CURRENTLY USE OR DO]
> Analyze the competitive landscape from the customer's perspective.
> 1. Identify direct competitors solving the same problem.
> 2. Identify indirect competitors solving the problem differently.
> 3. Identify products customers could use instead of my product.
> 4. Identify manual processes, spreadsheets, internal tools, agencies, employees, or habits competing with my solution.
> 5. Identify the strongest alternative that requires customers to do nothing.
> 6. Explain why a customer would switch from their current solution.
> 7. Identify where competitors are stronger than my idea.
> 8. Identify potential differentiation opportunities.
> 9. Identify features I should NOT build simply because competitors have them.
> 10. Define my strongest possible positioning against existing alternatives.
> Never accept “we have no competitors” as an answer.
> Treat the customer's current behavior as a competitor.
> Output: Direct → Indirect → Hidden Competitors → Strongest Threat → Weaknesses → Differentiation → Positioning
> 
> **Imagen 5**
> 4. GET YOUR FIRST 10 CUSTOMERS
> Creates a practical plan to find and convert the first 10 customers manually, before wasting money on ads or automation.
> Prompt:
> Act as an early stage growth strategist.
> Help me get my first 10 real customers using direct, manual outreach.
> STARTUP: [INSERT STARTUP IDEA]
> TARGET CUSTOMER: [INSERT IDEAL CUSTOMER]
> PROBLEM: [INSERT PROBLEM]
> PRODUCT: [INSERT PRODUCT OR MVP]
> PRICE: [INSERT PRICE]
> CURRENT TRACTION: [INSERT USERS, CUSTOMERS, REVENUE, OR “NONE”]
> MY ADVANTAGES: [INSERT NETWORK, EXPERTISE, AUDIENCE, OR EXPERIENCE]
> Create a practical plan to get my first 10 customers.
> 1. Identify where my ideal customers already spend time.
> 2. Rank the 5 best channels to reach them.
> 3. Create a simple manual outreach strategy.
> 4. Write 3 personalized messages focused on starting a conversation.
> 5. Define what to ask during the first conversation.
> 6. Identify the strongest buying signals.
> 7. Create a 14 day action plan with specific daily actions.
> Avoid paid ads and complicated automation unless clearly necessary.
> Focus on real conversations, customer learning, and getting the first 10 customers.
> Output: Where to Find Them → Best Channels → Outreach → Buying Signals → Follow Up → 14 Day Plan
> 
> **Imagen 6**
> 5. BUILD THE MVP
> Turns the idea into the smallest possible product that can test the most important business assumption with real users.
> Prompt:
> Act as an experienced product strategist helping me build a focused MVP.
> STARTUP IDEA: [INSERT STARTUP IDEA]
> TARGET CUSTOMER: [INSERT TARGET CUSTOMER]
> CORE PROBLEM: [INSERT PROBLEM]
> PROPOSED SOLUTION: [INSERT SOLUTION]
> CORE ASSUMPTION: [INSERT MOST IMPORTANT ASSUMPTION]
> RESOURCES: [INSERT TEAM, SKILLS, BUDGET, TECHNOLOGY, OR TIME]
> Analyze the idea and design the smallest MVP that can test the core assumption.
> 1. Identify the single assumption the MVP must prove.
> 2. Define the smallest product needed to test it.
> 3. List the essential features.
> 4. List what should be cut.
> 5. Define the user behavior that proves the hypothesis.
> 6. Define clear success and failure criteria.
> 7. Create a simple launch plan.
> 8. Create a 14 day execution roadmap.
> Do not design a complete product.
> Prioritize speed, learning, and real user behavior over polish.
> Output: Core Hypothesis → MVP → Must Have → Cut → Success Metric → Launch → 14 Day Plan
